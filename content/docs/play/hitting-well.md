A shot in Pelota is good when three things come together: **where you stand**, **when you
release the button** and **how much time the ball gives you**. This page is the player's guide;
[Hitting the ball](../../how-it-works/hitting/) explains the same things from the inside.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
By default the screen shows the **stroke indicator** (a circle above your player's head), the
landing ring of your aimed shot and the score panel. The ring at your feet, the shot ratings and
the players' stamina, pressure and confidence are turned on in **Options → Interface**.
{{< /callout >}}

## 1. Stand in the right place

When your opponent hits, a marker appears at your feet: a **ring** at the standard spot to meet
the ball, and a **line** through it. The line is the range of places you can take the ball from,
from early (closer to the net) to late (further back). The marker is **red** when you are far
off and turns **green** when you stand on the line.

{{< fig src="images/screenshots/training-moving-in.jpg" caption="Still on the way: the marker is orange. Get onto the line before you press the shot button." >}}

Once you press the shot button, your player only jogs. So do the running yourself, and press the
button when you are (nearly) there.

### Early or late? Your choice

Where on the line you stand decides how you take the ball:

- **Step in** and you take the ball **early**, on the rise. You borrow pace from your opponent's
  ball and give them less time, but the timing is harder (the perfect window is shorter) and you
  risk a little control.
- **Stay back** and you take it **late**, as it drops low. The timing is easier and safer, but the
  shot has less bite.

A normal groundstroke never needs more than about a meter of moving back. If the ball bounces
deep and high, step in rather than chasing it back: you take it higher, but on time.

## 2. Release at the right moment

Release the shot button **just before** the racket meets the ball. Within the last ~0.12 seconds
(longer for players with good timing, shorter when you take the ball early) your timing is
**perfect**. Release earlier and the shot loses accuracy, pace and spin step by step.

A perfect release is worth a lot. Compared with a release just outside the perfect window, a
perfect shot gets:

- **full pace**: the shot keeps all of its speed (92% when just outside the window), and its
  attacking pace is doubled;
- **much more spin**: 130% instead of about 105%;
- **far more precision**: the landing area shrinks from about 0.9 m to 0.25 m.

The **stroke indicator** above your head shows your timing. It appears as soon as the shot
button can be used (the opponent's ball comes in, or the serve is tossed) and goes from **red**
(releasing now would be too early) through **amber** to **green** when a release would be
perfect. Once you release it keeps the timing you got, and it disappears when the racket meets
the ball, or when the point is over. With the aim marker turned on, the landing ring on the far
side also shrinks as your timing gets better and **lights up** when a release would be perfect.

## 3. Use the time the ball gives you

The full attacking pace only comes through when you were **set early** (standing still for a
moment before contact) and the ball gave you time. A hard, fast ball or a deep ball near your
baseline rushes you: those shots come back with less pace and less control, whatever you do.
Against such balls, play safe and deep; attack the short and slow ones.

## The rating above your player

After each shot a word appears above your player:

| Rating | What it means |
|---|---|
| **PERFECT!** | Perfect timing *and* the ball in the racket's sweet spot |
| **GREAT** / **GOOD** | Both timing and position were fine |
| **EARLY** | You released the button too early |
| **LATE** | You were still holding the button at contact |
| **OUT OF POSITION** | You were too far from the ball |

The worse of timing and positioning decides the rating, and its name tells you which one to
work on.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Practice tip:** in training, stand still on the baseline and only work on the release. Once
PERFECT! comes regularly, start moving in and out to try early and late contact.
{{< /callout >}}
