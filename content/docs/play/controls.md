Directions always follow the screen: pushing **up** moves you up the screen, no matter which end
of the court you are on.

On a computer you play with **keyboard and mouse** or with the **keyboard only**: pick the
layout under **Options → Input → Keyboard Layout** (keyboard and mouse is the default).

| What | Keyboard & mouse | Keyboard only | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move | <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> or arrow keys | <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> or arrow keys | Left stick or D-pad |
| Aim | Mouse | <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> (during a shot) | Left stick (during a shot) |
| Topspin shot, flat serve | Left click | <kbd>J</kbd> | <kbd>A</kbd> |
| Slice shot, slice serve | Right click | <kbd>K</kbd> | <kbd>X</kbd> |
| Drop shot (drop volley at the net), kick serve | <kbd>Q</kbd> or middle click | <kbd>L</kbd> | <kbd>Y</kbd> |
| Ask the ball machine for a ball (training) | <kbd>E</kbd> | <kbd>E</kbd> | Right trigger |
| Pause / menu | <kbd>Esc</kbd> | <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Start |

## Rally: one button press, three jobs

When the opponent's ball comes, **press and hold** a shot button. From that moment:

1. **Your player moves to the ball on their own**, but only at a jogging pace. You should already
   be close: good footwork before you press is what makes the shot good. With keyboard and
   mouse, <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> keep moving you; let go of them
   and your player jogs to the ball on their own.
2. **You aim the shot.** A ring on the opponent's side shows where the ball will land.
   - **Mouse:** move the mouse; the ring follows it.
   - **Keyboard only:** the keys you hold pick one of nine zones: <kbd>A</kbd> or <kbd>D</kbd>
     a side, <kbd>W</kbd> deep, <kbd>S</kbd> short, two keys a corner, no key the middle. The
     zone you hold when you **release** the button counts, so you can let go of the keys after.
   - **Gamepad:** the stick aims instead of moving you, and the aim stays where you leave it
     when the stick springs back.
3. **Holding the button charges power.** About 0.8 seconds of holding gives the full attacking
   pace.

**Release the button just before the racket meets the ball.** That release is your timing:
the closer to contact, the better (see [Hitting well](../hitting-well/)). If you are still
holding the button when the racket meets the ball, the ball still goes the way you aim, but it
is weak: slower, shorter and less wide.

## Serve

1. Before the serve, left and right slide you along the baseline.
2. **Press and hold** a serve button to choose the serve: flat, slice or kick (see the table).
   While you hold it, you aim into the service box as for a shot.
3. **Release** to toss the ball. With the keyboard only, the zone you hold then counts.
4. **Press any serve button again right before the racket meets the ball.** The closer to the
   contact, the faster and more precise the serve. A serve you do not time is slow and wild.
